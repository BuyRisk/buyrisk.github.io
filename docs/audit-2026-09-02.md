# Site audit — 2 September 2026

Scope: every interactive tool (inputs, ranges, robustness, phone layout),
topic coverage against a beginner investing curriculum, and the reference
registry (bibliography, cited claims, and data-provider terms). Findings
that were fixed the same day are marked **fixed**; the rest are open.

## 1. Interactive tools

### Method

- Static read of all 79 React components and the shared stylesheets, cataloging
  every slider/number input (min, max, step, default, clamping).
- Browser sweep of every page and every tab at 375 px, measuring page-level
  horizontal overflow and the element responsible.
- "Extremes" probe: every range input on every tool driven to its max and then
  its min, scanning the rendered text for `NaN`, `Infinity`, `undefined` and
  watching for runtime errors.

### Fixed (commit 878f3a5)

| Area | What was wrong | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio lab | Typed Return/Risk/Correlation values were not finite-checked; clearing a field fed NaN into the covariance matrix and blanked every chart | Finite check + clamp (return ±50 %, risk 0.5–100 %, corr ±1) |
| Fees lab | All-minimum inputs rendered "NaN %" (fee share of a zero balance) | Guard on zero fee-free balance |
| Burn-rate lab | Same nest-egg state had min $100k in one mode and $0 in the other | Both $0 |
| Emergency fund | "Already saved" capped at $60k while the target can reach ~$187k, so "fully funded" was unreachable | Cap $250k |
| Live defaults | 10-yr yield, card APR and mortgage rate were off their slider's step grid; first touch jumped the value | Snapped to step |
| Ranges | Salary/portfolio/windfall/goal caps of $100k–$2M; ages capped at 60/70; horizons at 40 yr; card APR at 30 %; Roth contribution cap at the 2024 limit; Social Security PIA/survivor caps below the 2026 maximums; return floors at 3 % | Widened (see commit message for each) |
| Phone layout | Tooltip bubbles widened every tool page; debt-payoff rows, the rebalancing table, the diversification formula, the options lever charts and the portfolio per-asset inputs overflowed 375 px | `main` clips horizontal overflow; rows/tables scroll inside their cards; formula wraps; charts stack; stat grids drop to two columns |

After the fixes the extremes probe is clean on every tool and no page has
horizontal overflow at 375 px.

### Open (judgment calls, not bugs)

- **"7 %" means different things across tools.** Asset-location and tax-drag
  sliders are nominal; fees and rent-vs-buy are unlabeled; net-worth's tooltip
  says real but the projection reads nominal; compound growth is 10 % nominal
  with explicit inflation; savings-rate/Roth/Social Security are explicitly
  real. Recommend labeling every return slider "(nominal)" or "(real)" and
  standardizing on two defaults.
- **Default ordinary tax rate** is 32 % in asset-location/tax-drag but 22–24 %
  in Roth, harvest/convert and Social Security. 32 % implies ~$200k single.
- **Default lifecycle** in compound growth is age 20 + 30 years (retire at 50);
  everywhere else implies 65. Suggest 25 + 40.
- **Risk-free rate** is 3 % in CAPM/portfolio but 4 % in options/asset-location;
  could all read the live T-bill.
- **Filing status**: the three tax tools offer Single/MFJ only. Documented as
  intentional in the marginal-rate note, not in the Social Security panel.
- **Currency formatting**: marginal-rate lab bypasses `formatUsd` and so ignores
  the header currency picker.
- **Debt-payoff rows** scroll horizontally on phones; a two-row wrapped layout
  would be nicer.
- Rebalancing table rows are clickable but not keyboard-reachable.

## 2. Topic coverage

Covered well (a tool or reference page exists): risk & return, CAPM and
factors, compounding, savings rate, cost of debt and multi-debt payoff, fees,
inflation, tax drag, asset location, diversification and correlation, index
concentration, how many stocks, superstocks, DCF valuation, options,
efficient frontier, stock/bond split, bonds and rate risk, rebalancing, market
efficiency, market timing, lump sum vs DCA, active vs index, closet indexing,
fund flows, behavioral finance and the Bias Arcade, retirement spending and
sequence risk, Social Security claiming, Roth vs traditional, harvesting vs
converting, marginal rates, the next-dollar ladder (incl. HSA), budgeting,
emergency fund, savings goals, net worth, home bias, US vs world, currency
risk, rent vs buy, risk tolerance, leverage and volatility drag, tax-loss
harvesting, yields, CAPE, contribution limits, glossary.

Gaps, in suggested priority order:

1. **What to actually buy** — target-date funds, glide paths, the three-fund
   portfolio. Only one passing mention of target-date funds. This is the step a
   beginner is left without after the theory.
2. **Employer stock concentration** — RSUs, ESPPs, company stock in a 401(k).
   Directly the site's thesis (idiosyncratic risk earns nothing) applied to the
   most common concentrated position people hold.
3. **Speculation vs investing** — crypto (zero mentions), gold, collectibles:
   assets with no expected cash flow. Beginners will ask.
4. **Mortgage prepayment vs investing** (zero mentions) — natural extension of
   the cost-of-debt and rent-vs-buy tools.
5. **Withdrawal order and RMDs** — which account to draw first; only a passing
   mention in the burn-rate lab.
6. **Dividends** — the "dividends are free money" misconception has no dedicated
   treatment despite eleven passing mentions.
7. **Scams and "returns without risk"** — Ponzi/affinity fraud as the corollary
   of the site's thesis.
8. **Advisors and their fees** — fee-only vs AUM, and how a 1 % AUM fee lands in
   the fees lab.
9. Smaller: 529 plans, annuities/longevity insurance, REITs, credit risk in
   bonds, insurance basics.

## 3. Data provenance and licensing

Verified independently on 2 September 2026:

- The public repo tracks no raw licensed file (57 tracked data files, all
  FRED/French/Damodaran/Shiller/SSA or manifests). Nothing under `public/` but
  favicon, CNAME and security.txt.
- Every generated file derived from WRDS sources is aggregate-level except:
  `sp500-concentration.ts` (54 real tickers with index weights at 11
  month-ends, plus a monthly reconstructed cap-weighted S&P 500 series that
  matches CRSP's index at 0.9999 correlation); `fund-overlap.ts` (12
  anonymized per-fund rows, ordered by size with exact assets); and
  `behavior-gap.ts` (6 anonymized per-fund case rows). All three are already
  disclosed on the site as pending written confirmation of licence scope.
- **The history squash did not purge the old names from GitHub.** Closed
  Dependabot pull requests #2, #3, #4, #12 and #13 (and likely #6 and #10)
  were opened against the pre-squash `main`, and GitHub keeps
  `refs/pull/N/head` after a PR is closed. Fetching `refs/pull/2/head` from
  `origin` returns a tree whose `fund-overlap.ts` still contains the real fund
  names. Exposure is low (one must know to fetch a PR ref or a commit SHA),
  but it is reachable. The only remedy is a GitHub Support request to delete
  those refs and run garbage collection.
- The committed `crsp-*.ts` aggregates derive from a CRSP vintage that is no
  longer on disk (the manifest records this); they are not reproducible from
  the library as it stands until a fresh pull.
- Provider terms (fixed in commit 5818598): CRSP attribution line added; AQR's
  no-republishing clause stated accurately; FRED series cited to originators;
  the Case-Shiller annual index levels removed from the shipped housing data
  (FRED marks the series pre-approval-required; only three headline statistics
  now ship); attribution notes added for French, Shiller, Damodaran, SSA,
  FTSE Russell, ICI, SPIVA, JST, Pástor–Stambaugh and N-SAR.
- Open: `src/lib/nsarFlows.ts` has no reducer in the repo and its source folder
  mixes a Kellogg-built database with WRDS-style extracts, so which produced
  the shipped numbers cannot be confirmed from the repo alone. The
  `src/data/generated/README.md` lists only the two `crsp-*.ts` files as
  licensed-derived; five more are. The Bogleheads wiki licence could not be
  verified by automated fetch.

## 4. Bibliography and cited claims

Every quantitative claim attributed to a study or to market history was
checked against the source (or the site's own shipped data). Most held:
Barber & Odean's 6.5 pp/yr, Odean's disposition figures, Asch's 75 %, the
framing-effect swing, all four hindsight-episode facts, the 2022 Treasury
loss, the SPIVA year-end-2025 transcription, the ICI fee series, the 2016
N-SAR net-outflow year, Social Security claiming factors, Petajisto's 60 %
threshold, and the Bengen worst-cohort years.
