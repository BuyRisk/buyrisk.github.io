There's an old line among investors: you don't get paid for the return you
*want*. You get paid for the risk you're willing to *hold*. That idea is the
whole reason this site is called **Buy Risk**.

## Return is the price of discomfort

Imagine two ways to store $10,000 for a year.

- Leave it in an insured savings account. You know almost exactly what you'll
  have in twelve months.
- Buy a diversified basket of stocks. You have no idea what you'll have. It
  could be $8,500 or $12,500.

The second option, on average and over long stretches, has paid more. Not
because the market is generous, but because most people dislike uncertainty.
To get them to hold the uncertain asset, it has to offer a *higher expected
reward*. That extra expected reward is called the **risk premium**.

> For risk that can't be diversified away, risk and expected return are two
> sides of the same coin. For risk that can, they come apart completely.

## Not every risk is paid for

This is the qualification that does most of the work, and it's where the slogan
needs care. Only risk that **cannot be diversified away** carries an expected
premium.

Market risk — a crash, inflation, a recession — hits everything at once. No
amount of spreading your money around removes it, and since somebody has to
hold it, holders are compensated. This is **systematic** risk.

The risk that one company fails — a bad product, a fraud, a lost lawsuit — is
different. You can remove almost all of it by owning hundreds of companies
instead of five, and doing so costs essentially nothing. Because anyone can
shed it for free, no premium is offered for holding it. This is
**idiosyncratic** risk, and it is *uncompensated*.

The practical consequence is sharp. Putting your money in five stocks rather
than a broad index makes your outcome far more uncertain **without raising
what you should expect to earn**. You will hear about the concentrated
portfolios that beat the market. Those are the right-hand tail of a
distribution that got wider, not evidence of a premium being collected — and a
wider distribution has a left-hand tail of exactly the same origin.

So "buy risk" is really *buy the compensated risk, and diversify the rest
away*.

## "Buying risk" is a deliberate act

When you invest, you are choosing to take on a specific bundle of
undiversifiable risks (market volatility, inflation, interest rates) in
exchange for an expected premium. Done thoughtfully, that's not gambling. It's
a priced, repeatable trade.

The skill of investing is mostly about **which** risks to buy, **how much**,
and for **how long**, not about avoiding risk altogether. A portfolio with no
risk also has no risk premium, and inflation erodes it.
